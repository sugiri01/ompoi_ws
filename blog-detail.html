Understanding Price Spreads in Cashew Supply Chains

Cashew pricing in India varies dramatically across regions, quality grades, and seasons. Understanding these price spreads is essential for both buyers and sellers to make informed decisions and negotiate fair deals. Price spreads in cashew supply chains are influenced by multiple factors including regional production variations, quality grading systems, processing costs, transportation logistics, seasonal demand patterns, and export market dynamics. In Kerala, where cashew processing is concentrated, prices tend to be higher due to better infrastructure and proximity to export hubs. In contrast, tribal areas in Jharkhand and Odisha often see lower farmgate prices despite growing quality raw cashews.

Data consistency drives price discovery. When both buyers and sellers have access to market intelligence, transactions become faster, fairer, and more efficient.

Market Intelligence Insight

Cashew kernels are graded based on size, color, and wholeness. W180 (largest whole) commands premium prices, while W320 and W450 (smaller grades) are more affordable. Split and broken grades sell at significant discounts. Quality-adjusted pricing is critical — a W180 kernel might cost ₹800-900/kg while W450 ranges ₹600-700/kg. Without standardized grading, buyers and sellers struggle to find fair price points.

How Data Transparency Helps

OMPOI's pricing intelligence provides visibility into market rates across regions and quality grades. By aggregating transaction data, we help buyers identify fair prices and sellers price competitively. Our platform shows historical price bands, seasonal patterns, and regional variations — enabling both parties to negotiate from informed positions rather than guesswork. Understanding price spreads in cashew supply chains requires knowledge of regional dynamics, quality standards, and market trends.

  • Always specify quality grade clearly in RFQs to get accurate quotes
  • Compare prices across multiple suppliers and regions before committing
  • Use historical data to understand seasonal price fluctuations
